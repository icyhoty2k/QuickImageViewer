1. Aspect Ratio and Shape GeometryAn arrow is a directional pointer, meaning it is naturally tall and slender rather than square. If you forced the graphic to stretch all the way to x=63, y=63 to fill the whole box, it would double in width and turn into a massive, wide, chunky wedge instead of a sharp, elegant cursor. Preserving that ~32x50 proportion keeps the classic, recognizable angle of the arrow intact.2. UI Occlusion (Visibility)If the cursor graphic filled a full 64x64 solid block, it would cover up significantly more of the screen. A wider, bulkier arrow would block letters, text, and small UI buttons right next to your click target, making precision work much harder.3. Scaling Consistency from the 32x32 StandardThe classic 32x32 cursor graphic originally occupied roughly a 16x25 pixel area inside its canvas. When Microsoft created the high-resolution 64x64 (_l) variant, they scaled the geometry up by an exact $2\times$ factor (roughly $16 \times 2 = 32$ wide, and $25 \times 2 = 50$ tall).The extra space surrounding that 32x50 shape isn't an oversight—it is the necessary canvas container that allows the arrow to maintain its precise shape, proper angle, and smooth anti-aliased drop shadows without getting clipped at the edges.
