Cull a photo shoot on Windows — one key per picture

Keep or reject with a single keystroke, and the viewer moves to the next frame by itself. Four hundred pictures is four hundred keys and no mouse. Free, and nothing to install.

After a shoot the slow part is not choosing — it is the clicking around the choosing. Dedicated culling tools are paid, and a general viewer makes you delete or drag each file by hand. QuickImageViewer has a cull mode built in: mark as fast as you can look, decide what happens to the files once, at the end.

How it works

KeyWhat it does
Alt+SpaceEnter or leave cull mode
KKeep, and advance
XReject, and advance
UClear the mark, and advance

While the mode is on, the filename in the corner carries its mark: a tick for keep, a cross for reject, a dot for a frame you have not judged yet.

Nothing touches the disk while you mark

Marks live in memory. Change your mind as often as you like — only leaving the mode asks what to do, and saying no moves nothing.

Saying yes moves, never deletes: the files go into a _rejected folder beside them, on the same drive, and Ctrl+Z in Explorer puts them back.

Reject the bad ones, or keep the good ones

Whichever mark you used is the one that acts. Mark rejects and those files move. Mark only keeps and you are asked whether to move everything else — which is how four hundred frames become the twelve good ones without rejecting the other 388 one at a time.

Forty at once, from a thumbnail strip

While cull mode is on, every thumbnail strip tints its pictures green for keep and red for reject, and its right-click menu gains Mark Keep, Mark Reject and Clear Mark. Select a run of near-identical burst frames and mark them in one go; the keyboard handles the picture on screen.

Why it is fast enough to cull with

Decoded pictures are kept on the graphics card and the neighbours either side are decoded ahead of time, so the next frame is already there when you press the key. Culling is exactly the job where a visible load between pictures adds up.

What it is not

⚠ It is a viewer with a cull mode, not a catalogue. There are no star ratings written into the files, no colour labels and no Lightroom sync. If your workflow lives in a catalogue, cull there. If you want a folder of four hundred frames down to the keepers before anything else opens, this is the quick way.

Questions

Which files can it cull?

Anything it opens: JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF, TIFF, AVIF, HEIC and JPEG XL among them. Camera RAW (.dng, .cr2, .cr3, .nef, .arw) and HEIC need Microsoft's free Raw Image and HEIF extensions, because those go through the Windows imaging pipeline. The full format table →

What does it cost?

Nothing. QuickImageViewer is free and open source under the AGPL — no advertising, no telemetry, nothing to buy, no account.

Do I have to install it?

No. One portable executable, no installer, no Visual C++ Redistributable. It is also on winget: winget install IcyHotY2K.QuickImageViewer.
